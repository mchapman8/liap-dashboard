Story 1

CE Lab outage: ~150 test machines in a day

Drives ResultsCollaboratesDecision QualitySituational Adaptability
S

SituationDuring my Career Experience running the watchOS Automation Lab, my morning AI triage tool flagged an unusual spike of test machines failing overnight. An automated update had moved a large group onto an unreleased OS build, and testing for the watchOS engineering teams was stalled.

T

TaskGet the machines back online fast enough to limit the testing impact, even though every affected Mac had to be physically handled.

A
Action
  • Took the findings to my manager and a peer lab manager, and we worked out a fix together.
  • Brought in the full team to talk through the impact and how to limit it.
  • Split the work by access: I was the only one with lab access, so I physically pulled and updated each Mac while a teammate finished setup remotely.
  • Stayed in constant contact on each machine's status, and sent the wider team regular updates on how many were back online and how many were left.
R

ResultReplaced nearly 150 machines in one day, fully resolved within two. We traced the root cause to a staging update applied to the full lab, and the team added a confirmation step before changes that large could go through.

"The fix was only half of it. Keeping everyone informed is what kept testing moving."

Say it plainly: "test machines," not agents. "Automated update," not playbooks. "Affected," not infected. Another engineer added the confirmation step, so credit the team.

Story 3

Device lifecycle document

Builds Effective TeamsDevelops TalentCustomer FocusPlans & Aligns
S

SituationI noticed a trend of devices being checked in incorrectly: missing photos, notes unrelated to the issue, names not verified before signing. Each was minor, but a few came back as larger problems.

T

TaskWhen I was asked to lead our Genius Bar split download, I chose this as the topic, aiming to fix the habit and not just the moment.

A
Action
  • Co-presented with a fellow Genius. I identified the issue and proposed a living document mapping a device's lifecycle while it's with us.
  • Focused on the bare-minimum steps at each stage and why each matters, like Connect and Personalize at check-in and mandatory photos on every device.
  • My partner helped build the outline, and the team's feedback during the download built the core of the document together.
R

ResultImmediate improvement that day, and positive feedback from technicians and leadership. It has slipped since, so I'm finishing the document and pinning it to the Genius Bar team's Slack, where anyone can check it and we keep it current.

"A download fades, but a reference doesn't."

Before 2pm: if it's pinned, say "it's now pinned." Also a fallback for "something that didn't go as planned."
Story 4

Mobile repair strategy and Mac completion

Drives Results (sustained)Customer FocusInstills TrustCollaborates
S

SituationRepair turnaround averaged about 90 minutes, and the technician culture was more reactive than proactive.

T

TaskBring turnaround down without sacrificing quality, and keep Mac in-store completion above the 75% company goal.

A
Action
  • Designed a mobile repair strategy with management, shifting toward proactive repair and full customer-journey support.
  • Some senior technicians pushed back. I explained the why: faster turnaround improves the customer's experience directly and frees capacity to support more customers.
  • Coached Technical Experts on "early and often": flag a repair as soon as it slips past 90 minutes and raise issues early. I made sure those conversations reached the right people; I didn't move resources myself.
  • Set consistent customer expectations and kept open communication with Geniuses and leadership.
R

ResultTurnaround went from 90 to under 60 minutes at a 99% success rate. Mac completion held at 80–90% against the 75% goal. Daily numbers move with demand and staffing, but the average stayed improved and the culture change held.

Tell it as leadership, not technique. You may have used this in round 1 under Functional Expertise. Lead with the pushback and the culture change.

Story 6

The MacBook repair with no notes

Manages ConflictDecision QualityInstills TrustBuilds Effective Teams
S

SituationOn a Sunday before open, a fellow Genius picked up a MacBook someone had started working on with no notes. We couldn't tell if parts were installed, if it had been opened, or if diagnostics or a revive had been done. He got frustrated right away.

T

TaskKeep his frustration from turning into blame, and make the right call on a repair we didn't have enough information about.

A
Action
  • Talked through reasons a repair might be left that way, and encouraged assuming positive intent until we had the full picture.
  • We agreed to set it aside until the Lead Genius or admin arrived, and moved on to other repairs.
  • About an hour later the admin explained that Repair Central wasn't saving anything new on that repair, and an IS&T ticket was already open.
  • Debriefed with the frustrated Genius. We agreed it was a one-off and that the fix was visibility: post these situations in Slack.
R

ResultSetting it aside was the right call. The frustrated Genius gave the original technician the Slack feedback himself, constructively, and the team got a clear practice for one-offs.

Strongest beat: the frustrated Genius delivered the feedback himself. You turned a potential clash into peer-to-peer coaching.
